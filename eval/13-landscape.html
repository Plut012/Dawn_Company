Eval 13 — Phone landscape layout

A phone held on its side (the screen is wider than it is tall and under about 500 px tall) now has its own layout on every screen. The board takes the left and the whole height; the kit is one column on the right. Same parts, same tokens, same look; only the placement changed. Portrait and laptop are as they were (section 5). Everything on this page was measured on the real client in headless Chromium with device emulation, real touch events and real screenshots; there is no physical phone (section 7).

Needs npx vite (port 5173) and, for the client frame, the game server (npm run server); the manual frame needs neither. The two keys below turn the frames, which is what turning a phone does: the page is not reloaded, only resized.

One render fix was needed for this. board.resize() kept the zoom, so the field manual had to rebuild the lesson (back to the start of the round) to re-frame its tiny maps; a turned phone lost its step. resize() now frames the map again by itself when the camera was still where the map was framed, and leaves it alone when the player has panned or zoomed (src/client/render/index.ts, isFramed), so the manual only calls resize().

5. Portrait and laptop

Compared with screenshots from before the change, every screen at 390 × 844 and at 1280 × 800: 52 of 56 pixel-identical; the other four were caught in mid-animation (a playback frame and the manual after Step, the two pairs below differ only by where the animation was at that instant). Element geometry (every box, to a tenth of a pixel) is identical on 58 of 58 screens; the eight that differ by name differ only in the new class builder on the squad panel. There is no deliberate difference. (The viewport meta now has viewport-fit=cover; safe areas are 0 without a notch, so nothing moves.)

6. Safe areas, four seats and the clock

With the safe area emulated at 47 px left and right and 21 px at the bottom (Emulation.setSafeAreaInsetsOverride, a notched phone in landscape), no control sits in the unsafe strips on the play screen; the page background shows in them. The board loses the strips, so at 844 it is 455 × 369 (54% wide): the cost of a notch. With four seats and a running clock the status plate holds three readouts and two rows of lamps, and the lever and the chits still fit.

7. Not verified, and what is still awkward

  • No physical phone. To check on a real one: the address bar and toolbar changing the visible height while the page is up (the play and manual screens use a fixed box and should follow; the lobby uses dvh); the notch's real insets and the home-indicator; thumb reach to the lever (top right of the kit column) and the chits (bottom); whether the report strip's text is comfortable at arm's length; the on-screen keyboard over the name field in landscape (the join screen is an ordinary scrolling page).
  • 667 × 375 is tight. The board is 60%; the chits are icons without words there (below 720 px wide), the ready lever is a little shorter (56 × 92 for 64 × 112; the same part, travelling the same way), and the squad list shows two cards at a time with four seats and the clock; it scrolls, and the selected unit stays in view.
  • The lobby is still a scrolling page: the lever, Start and the roll stay put at the left while the builder scrolls (the page is about 1700 px tall at 844 wide, four to five screens; it was 1816). It is the roster text (five units, each with its blurb and actions) that is long. Picking a preset, then the lever, needs no scroll.
  • The manual's lesson text scrolls inside its box on the longest lessons at 667 wide (the five lessons with "View as" most of all); the keys, the report line and Step never scroll. The lesson list is a drawer of 17 keys that scrolls.
  • A notched phone gets about 54% for the board at 844 (the kit column keeps its width; the safe strips are not used).
  • At 932 × 430 and wider the old wide layout used to apply and was cramped in height (one squad card); the wide layout is now for windows over 500 px tall.
