        2. The real play screen, on a scripted feed

        Three copies of eval/14-play-frame.html: the play screen itself, mounted with a stand-in for the socket. When the lever goes down the frame runs the real validateOrders and resolve and hands the screen the resolve message the server would send. The enemy (three riflemen at H3, G5, I6) holds every round; with Enemy: ambush the one at G5 lies in ambush instead. Each frame is its own match. A match that ends starts again.

        To order move-then-act by hand: pick a rifleman (say the one at B4), press Move, tap a marked hex (D4), press Fire (it is still lit, because the rifle may follow a move; the marked hexes are now those within rifle range of D4, not of B4), tap the enemy at G5. The card reads Move · Fire, the strip Move D4 · Fire G5, and the map shows the route and, from its end, the aim. Pull the lever. Things to try:

        • The medic at A5: Move to B5 (through or beside his own men), then Treat, then C5, where a man starts pinned.
        • With Move lit, tap B3 for the man at A3: the route ends on his teammate, and the strip says what will happen. Tap C3 instead: the route passes through B3.
        • The machine gunner at B6: after Move, his Fire chit replaces the move (a heavy weapon is not mobile).
        • Set move-then-fire, then tap the unit's own hex with Move lit: the move and its follow-up are both gone.
        • Set move-then-fire, then change the route so the target is out of range: the follow-up is dropped and the strip says so.
        • Aim a follow-up at a hex behind the woods (E1, D2): legal to order; in the assault phase the log says fire called off, no line.
        • Enemy: ambush, then walk a rifleman to D4 with a follow-up at G5: he is pinned on the way and the log says fire called off, pinned.

        6. What does not work, was not checked, or is awkward

        • Not checked: a real phone; the real client against the real server in a browser (the bots cover the server over sockets, the frames cover the screen); the look of a fall back as it animates (its events, the log lines and where everyone ends were checked, the animation was not watched frame by frame); Enemy: ambush in the frames by hand (the sandbox stage "Pinned on the way" covers the rule).
        • Fixed 2026-11-20: a plain shot after a move is pencilled in. The first press of Fire aims the follow-up; the strip says to press Fire again to shoot from where he stands, which replaces the move (checked by mouse in the frame at 1280 × 720 and 390 × 844). A third press puts the chit down.
        • Fixed 2026-11-20: follow-up marks. Only hexes where the follow-up would run as things stand are marked: within his sight this round with a clear line from the path's end, and for Treat a hex where a teammate will be (likelyFollowUps in src/client/ui/draft.ts). On the real maps a rifleman's move marks about 21–29 hexes instead of 34–41. The order rule is unchanged: sight is still judged when the move is over.
        • The unit card says "Move · Fire" without the hexes; a 176 px card on a phone has no room for more. The strip and the map have them.
        • A medic's follow-up at a teammate who is not pinned "runs" and does nothing, without an event. His action needs a friend on the hex, not a pinned one (that is the data, from task 12), so the "nothing to treat" line appears only when nobody is there at all.
        • An enemy who sees the unit is told when its follow-up did not happen (unable goes to whoever sees the unit, as blocked does). That tells him an action was ordered, though not at what.
        • Units of different squads can share a hex for a moment, when one falls back into a hex an enemy is walking through (121 such moments in 2,880 random rounds). And a unit pushed all the way back to its start hex takes it back from whoever has stopped there, enemy included, who then falls back along his own path. Both are needed for settling to always end; neither is something a player would expect.
        • A pinned man moves: one pinned by an ambush while standing in a teammate's hex falls back along his path.
        • An ambush shot at a hex two teammates share hits both, as the task asks. Walking through your own men in front of an ambusher costs two pins for one shot.
        • Two teammates walking the same route to the same last hex both stop one hex short, as asked; but that hex is one they share, so both fall back further, to wherever their routes last differed.
        • The step of a move event now counts the moments of movement in the round, fall backs included, so it is no longer the index of the step along the unit's path.
        • The manual's "orders marked" count tells a move's follow-up mark from another unit's target mark by where the aim starts; a unit firing from the very hex another's route ends on would be miscounted.
        • eval/11-field-manual.html still says seventeen lessons and five phases; it was left as the record of task 11.
