Eval 24 — Dig in

A unit that holds still for a whole round digs in: at the end of the round, in the score phase, after the returns, it is dug in and ignores one more hit a round than its ground gives it, until its first step. There is no order for it. Everything on this page runs on Vite alone (npx vite --port 5199 --strictPort from the repo root, then open /eval/24-dig-in.html); the board, the kit, the manual and every number below are the real ones, run when the page loaded. The type dig's event is in src/shared/types.ts, the rule in src/rules/resolve.ts (score phase) and world.shelter (the hit rule).

1. The real board: a man digs in, then steps out

Two rounds of the real resolve on a small field (seven by five open hexes): in round 1 Blue's two riflemen (B2, B4) hold still and Plum's two walk up two hexes; in round 2 Plum's riflemen fire at them, and Blue's B2 steps one hex out (C2) while B4 holds. Press Play round 1, then Play round 2: the sandbags come up round the two blue men at the end of round 1; in round 2 both shots find them in their holes (cover), then the man at B2 steps out and the ring stays behind and goes. The board is shown to the umpire (every unit).

Loading the board.

2. The hit rule, from the real resolve

One man of Plum on the ground named, shot at by 1 to 4 of Blue's riflemen (or by a sniper first), his foxhole as shown. Each cell is what the shots did, in order: cover = turned aside, pinned, down. The ground gives 0 or 1, the foxhole 1 more; a sniper's hit ignores both and uses neither up.

3. Giving it up, and not getting it

Also from the real resolve: one man of Plum dug in on C2, given the order named, with one to three shots from Blue's men at him. dug in after is his state at the round's end; digs is how many dig events the round told, whoever they were about.

4. The lesson, in the real manual

The frame is the real field manual (/?manual=dig-in), which runs the lesson through the real resolve: the same two shots at a man in the open and a man dug in. Press its Step key to watch; the table under it is checkLesson run on this page, and the number of lessons that hold.

Reading the figures. About 45 men in 100 are dug in at the start of a round, because the scripted players hold still about as often as they walk, and three in four of the hits that were turned aside (cover or foxhole) fell on a man who was dug in at the round's start. Fewer men go down (about one in ten), and the matches are not otherwise changed: income per seat round, win shares and the seat gaps stay within their noise (two players: seat 0 now +0.5 ± 2.6 against the other, win share 0.51 / 0.49; four: win shares 0.21 / 0.24 / 0.27 / 0.28). The scripts do not move out of a foxhole for being shot at, which a person would; a human squad that holds a line should be harder to shift than these figures say, and one that rushes should lose nothing. Not tuned: whether one extra hit is too much or too little is for people to say.

The seeded digests are new (src/rules/digest.test.ts, combined 29aa3da7c80cd1b4 and, for Last Stand, f54edefd5efd695e), and were proved first: with the digging switched off and dugIn left out of the digest, all sixty came out as task 23's (b712e4916b5b1407 and d4e099948cd03368).

What does not work, or was not looked at

  • The sandbags were looked at in headless Chromium (WebGL, software rendering) on the small field of Part 1 at one camera framing, and in the manual frame: standing riflemen only. A pinned or down man inside the ring, the other four unit types, a ring on a slope or beside a wall or building, and a phone screen were not looked at (the unit tests check that the ring has eleven bags, stays inside the hex and is low).
  • Leaving the foxhole is instant on the board: the ring is taken off before the first step, with no animation of its own (digging in has one).
  • The play screen was run only through the unit tests of its helpers (ground.test.ts, lines.test.ts) and the bots (the real protocol); the card line (“Dug in”), the hex description, the chits' tooltip for Move and Dash and the order line were not looked at in a real browser session with a server. Nothing on the phone layout was changed or tried.
  • A dug-in man whom the sniper shoots, or who is shot by a mortar, behaves as the rule says (the sniper ignores the foxhole; the mortar's round is an ordinary hit); the manual has no lesson for either.
  • The report line “Dug in: A, B, C.” lists every man who dug in that round that the viewer sees, which with a full squad is a long line each round; it is one line, but it is noise. Not changed.
  • The AI players do nothing to use it: no bot holds still on purpose.
