Eval 29 — Diorama: the rest of the squad

Dawn Company's diorama kit gets the rest of the squad: its own gunner, sniper, mortar, medic and engineer, of one set with the rifleman (src/client/render/kits/diorama/units.ts). No unit type falls back to lowpoly any more. Both boards below are the real createBoard, each with its own kit, showing the same PlayerView. Runs on Vite alone (npx vite --port 5198 --strictPort, then /eval/29-diorama-units.html).

1. Side by side

The line-up. A hand-made map, thirteen by five. Across each block the six types stand in the same order: rifleman, gunner, sniper, mortar, medic, engineer. Blue (bar), columns A to F, on open ground: row 1 ready, row 2 pinned, row 3 down, row 4 dug in. Plum (chevron), columns H to M: row 1 ready on open ground, row 2 ready in the crops, row 3 pinned in the woods, row 4 down. A Blue medic stands in the house at G2 (open round him). Row 5 is for the playback: a Blue rifleman (A5), gunner (C5), sniper (D5), mortar (E5), a pinned rifleman (F5) with a medic beside him (G5), an engineer (I5), and a Plum rifleman far off at M5. The state is createMatch's and the view viewFor's for Blue, then changed by hand (statuses set, every unit shown). Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom: each board has its own camera.

4. Notes: what was judged, what was not, what falls short

What was built. One rig for every man of the kit (the rifleman's: hips, trunk with arms and what he carries, head, two thighs, two shins, each a merged, painted geometry), and a table of figures. A type is its trunk (built once per kind: the shared chest, arms bent to wherever the hands must be, and what he holds), its head (plain, scrimmed, or the medic's) and its three poses. Legs, hips, base, burst, foxhole and the rig lowpoly's effects drive are the same for all.

  • Gunner: belly down behind a light machine gun, propped on his elbows, legs apart; the gun runs on past his head to a bipod, a magazine standing on it. He lies across the base with the gun to the right of the picture, so its length shows. Pinned, his face goes into the dirt; down, he is on his back.
  • Sniper: kneeling, a long scoped rifle levelled to the right; hessian scrim on his helmet, shoulders and back. Pinned, he doubles over it; down, he lies on his side (the rig gained a sideways roll for this: on his back the rifle pointed at the sky).
  • Mortar: two men on the one base, kneeling either side of the tube: number one with both hands up at the muzzle, number two with a round held ready. The tube, baseplate and bipod are one more geometry. Pinned, both duck; down, both lie, and the tube stands.
  • Medic: standing, no weapon; a satchel on each hip with crossed straps, an off-white helmet with a small dull cross on its crown, an armband. The cross is #8a4a3e, less saturated than Rust, and a test keeps it under 3% of the figure's paint.
  • Engineer: standing, no weapon; a shovel over the right shoulder with its blade behind his head, a coil of wire hung from the left shoulder, a sandbag in his left hand.

Cost per figure (counted): a one-man figure is 7 draw calls for the man and 5 for the base (as the rifleman was), plus the burst (2) while pinned and the foxhole (1) while dug in; the mortar is 15 for its two men and tube, and the same base. Triangles, everything on the base included: rifleman 5,460, medic 5,440, gunner 5,650, engineer 5,680, sniper 6,830, mortar 8,890. There is no cost constant: nothing here scales with a setting, and a squad is at most eight units.

Judged from the stills (headless Chromium, software WebGL, device pixel ratio 1).

  • Close in at dawn 1 every type is plainly itself and of one set with the rifleman: same paint, same scale, same base.
  • At the whole line-up on a desktop (about 95 px a hex) each type reads by shape: the lying man with the long gun, the kneeling man with the long rifle, the pair at the tube, the pale helmet, the shovel's blade over a shoulder.
  • At phone size at dawn 0 (about 60 px a hex) the medic (pale helmet), the engineer (blade above the head), the mortar (two men) and the sniper (kneeling, rifle) read; the rifleman reads by not being any of them.
  • Playback: the frames caught mid-round show the paper rings of the medic's aid round him and the man getting up, the tracers of the burst and the shot, and the puff at the mortar's muzzle; the still after the round shows the rifleman moved, the man at F5 on his feet and sandbags at J5.
  • Lowpoly boards of eval 26 and 28 taken now are byte for byte the committed stills.

Where it falls short.

  • The gunner in the mist. He lies low, so at dawn 0 the mist takes most of him: at phone size a ready gunner is a pale lying shape, close to a man who is down (who is slate, and has no gun pointing out). The gun's dark line and the missing fade tell them apart close in; at phone size in a thick bank it is the weakest read of the six.
  • He overhangs his base: a man lying behind a machine gun is 1.6 long and the base 1.3 across; his boots and the muzzle are over the rim by about 0.1 each.
  • The mortar's base is crowded: two men and a tube in 1.3; from the board's height they are one dark cluster with a tube in it, and when both are down they lie on each other.
  • Medic and engineer are the rifleman's body with other things on it. Their silhouettes differ from his by a helmet's colour and a blade over a shoulder; the coil and the satchels only show close in.
  • The sniper is not low: kneeling with scrim he is 0.95 tall against the rifleman's 1.1. Prone would have been lower but is the gunner's shape.
  • Men who move do not walk: a prone gunner slides along the ground as he is, a kneeling crew slides kneeling. The poses blend between statuses, not through a stride.
  • In woods the long figures (gunner, sniper) reach into the trunks at the sides of the hex.
  • The bipod's feet, a kneeling man's toe and a ducking sniper's muzzle go a little into the base.

Not verified. A real phone and frame rate; any motion (a hit, a stand, a dash's lean on the new figures were exercised through the rig in the tests, and seen only as stills before and after); a real match with a server; spotted and lost fades on the new figures; the figures on levels 1 and 2; device pixel ratio 2 and 3, where the small reads would be sharper.

Deviations from the task doc. (1) The pose table gained a sideways roll (used by the downed sniper). (2) The sniper kneels rather than lies, to keep him apart from the gunner. (3) The eval's line-up has Plum's four rows on four grounds (open, crops, woods, open) rather than every state on every ground.
