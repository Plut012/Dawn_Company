Eval 25 — Engineer and works

The engineer (Spr., cost ?, at most ? a squad, Supply Line only) has one special, Build: sandbags, wire or an MG nest on his own hex or the next one, paid from supply at the round's end, before the captures. A work is ground: it replaces the hex's terrain in the match's own copy of the map, for everyone, for the rest of the match. Costs now: ? supply; a rifleman's buy-back is ?.

1. Two real clients: an engineer builds each work in turn

A match is set up by the real rules (scripts/works-log.ts: the seeded script plays Ann's Pioneer section against Bo's Line section on ford until Ann has an engineer on his feet, outside every objective, with free ground round him and the supply for all three works) and written as the server's order log. The server resumes it and the two frames take their seats back with the log's tokens. Leave 8080 and 5173 alone; every run needs a fresh log (the server appends to it):

mkdir -p /tmp/ds25 && npx tsx scripts/works-log.ts /tmp/ds25/match.jsonl      # writes the log, names the engineer
npx tsx src/server/main.ts --port 8090 --timer 0 --resume /tmp/ds25/match.jsonl --matches /tmp/ds25
DS_SERVER_PORT=8090 npx vite --port 5190 --strictPort
# then open  http://localhost:5190/eval/25-engineer-and-works.html

Frame A is Ann (seat 0, the engineer's side), frame B is Bo, who only pulls his lever (his men hold). Run the walk-through works the frames through their own DOM: it picks the engineer's card and the Build chit, taps hexes on the real board (pointer events on its canvas, at points found by tapping round the engineer and reading which hex the report strip names), presses the work keys, pulls both levers and reads back what each screen shows, for three rounds: sandbags, then wire, then an MG nest, each on a free hex next to him. You can do the same by hand in the frames.

    5. What does not work, or was not verified

    • Works are never destroyed (out of scope): a wire line laid across a ford stays for the match.
    • A seat learns of every work on the map, in sight or not: the view's map carries the terrain, as the task says ("changes the hex for everyone"); only the build event is told just to the owner and whoever sees the hex.
    • A man already down may lie on a hex that wire is later put over. He comes back at his HQ, so nothing hangs on it, but the board draws nothing there (a down man has no piece).
    • The work keys grey out wire on a hex where a man stands or means to end up, as far as the planning player knows; an enemy who walks in makes it blocked when it lands.
    • The seeded script never orders a work it cannot pay for, so its buildNot_supply is 0; the bots and the property test do, and see supply refusals.
    • The figures say the script builds about 2.5 works an engineer a match and that its works take few hits (0.38 a match): the script does not stand behind them on purpose. Whether a work is worth its cost against a buy-back is for real play to show.
    • No physical phone was tried; the work keys take the chit row's place, which fits four keys at 667 × 375 only in the emulated layout.
