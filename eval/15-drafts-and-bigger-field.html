Eval 15 — Unsent orders count, and a bigger field

Two changes. Part 1: unsent orders go through (needs a game server). Part 2: the 17 × 13 field, squads of 20 points and 15 rounds (needs only Vite: everything in it is drawn from the real content and the real board).

1. Unsent orders go through

Start a game server on a spare port with a short clock and point Vite at it. These two commands leave a server already on 8080 and the usual Vite on 5173 alone (use another Vite port if 5173 is taken):

npx tsx src/server/main.ts --port 8095 --timer 20 --matches /tmp/ds15-matches
DS_SERVER_PORT=8095 npx vite --port 5182 --strictPort
# then open  http://localhost:5182/eval/15-drafts-and-bigger-field.html

The two frames are the real client (/?slot=dev15a and dev15b), each holding its own seat. Seat A is the one that draws and never pulls the lever; seat B pulls its lever at once, so the only thing that can end the round is the bell. The page only seats the frames and reloads them; it reaches into nothing else.

Press Seat both, then Start in seat A (the host).

    2. A bigger field and bigger squads

    Everything here is read from src/content when the page loads: the map, the budget, the presets, the round count. The board is the real board; the plan and the table are drawn from the same MAPS.crossroads.

    The numbers

    The map on the real board

    Four squads on their start lines (the Line, Weapons, Scout and Line sections), everything in sight. The rings mark the 32 start hexes. Drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom; it opens on the whole map.

    The plan

    The same map as a flat plan. Numbers on the coloured rings are the start hexes in placement order (1 nearest the centre), one colour per start zone; amber discs are objectives with their value; a small digit in the corner of a hex is its level (1 or 2). Hatched hexes are not walkable (water).

    Measured step distances from each start zone

    Walked over passable hexes one step at a time, as a unit moves (only water is in the way), with the same breadth-first search the content test uses. A rifleman moves 2 hexes a round, so rounds = steps divided by 2, rounded up.

    Cover on the way in

    The way each start zone takes to its own hamlet (a 2), found three times over: the cheapest walk that prefers hiding ground, then again with the hexes of the first walks closed, then once more. Hiding ground (woods, crops, a building) hides a walking unit until an enemy is within 2 hexes. “In sight of the centre” is a clear line from the centre hex, within 6.

    Seat fairness and match length

    Numbers from real runs, 2026-11-18. The commands are in the table caption so they can be repeated.

    Framing the board

    The real play screen, a match in progress, opened fresh at each size (headless Chromium over CDP, a real server on a spare port, two seated clients; this is seat 0, the north-west start zone). At 1280 × 800 the whole map is in frame. On the phone sizes the board opens at its smallest comfortable scale (28 px to a world unit): in portrait the whole height of the map is in frame and the width runs off to the east, in landscape the north-west corner fills the board; either can be panned and zoomed out to the whole map. The hex labels were made taller (0.26 to 0.44) so that they are readable at that scale.

    Not shown, or not working

    • The held draft is lost if the server process is restarted (a resumed match replays the log, which holds only the orders that ran).
    • Edits made while the line is down never reach the server, and the plan that comes with the reconnect replaces them with what the server held.
    • Part 2. Three players is not fair: the seat in the north-east corner (the middle of the three zones, opposite the empty one) scores 4 to 5 points a match less than the others in the seeded runs above, and wins about 0.18 of the matches where 0.33 would be even. Nothing in the map fixes that; see the open items in docs/tasks/00-plan.md.
    • Part 2. Two and four players show a small edge for the north-west zone (about a point of 8 to 14) that follows the zone, not the seat. Line of sight is not exactly symmetric on a symmetric map (the hex line breaks ties with a fixed nudge: 3.8% of hex pairs within 6 disagree under a north-south mirror, 5.9% under a half turn), which is the likely cause; not proven, and not changed (it is a rules matter).
    • Part 2. The Scout section had to change from the suggested 6 riflemen, a sniper and 2 medics: that is 9 units and the limit is 8. Spending 20 points on 8 units needs at least one heavier unit, so it is 5 riflemen, a sniper, a machine gunner and a medic.
    • Part 2. The framing shots were taken in headless Chromium with software GL; no real phone was used. On the phone sizes only part of the map is in frame at the opening scale (the board then moves to your squad); the whole map is one pinch-out away. Pan and zoom were driven with synthetic mouse events only, not touch.
    • An order the server rejects at the lever stays on screen (the strip says why); the server still holds the last valid draft until the next change is sent, so a bell at that moment runs the older valid draft.
