Eval 26 — Diorama trial

A trial of another visual direction, as a second kit beside lowpoly: the world is real, the information is drawn. The diorama kit (src/client/render/kits/diorama/) made three things itself in this task: the light and air, open ground, and the rifleman. Since task 27 it also makes woods and buildings (see eval/27-diorama-woods-and-buildings.html), so the live boards below now show the diorama's own trees and house; the stills on this page are task 26's and still show lowpoly's. Crops, water, the works and the other units are lowpoly's, asked for call by call. Both boards below are the real createBoard, each with its own kit, showing the same PlayerView. Runs on Vite alone (npx vite --port 5199 --strictPort, then /eval/26-diorama-trial.html).

4. Notes: what was judged, what was not, what falls short

Second pass (the direction approved): the mist becomes air, the bases calm down.

  • The mist is now sheets in the air, over a thin haze on surfaces. Nine thin, gently rolling sheets (MIST_LAYERS.desktop; four on a phone) lie between 0.06 and 0.74 above level-0 ground. Each is cut into banks and gaps by the noise texture in world space, offset by a seed made from the map's hexes; a higher sheet keeps only the heart of each bank, so a bank is a mound. The whole depth is shared out among the sheets, so the phone setting is coarser, not thinner. They are drawn after everything solid, so they lie in front of whatever stands in them. Level 2 (1.0) is above every sheet; level 1 (0.5) has two thin ones over it at dawn 0 and none from about dawn 0.5; at dawn 1 three low sheets with wide gaps are left. Sheets above the mist's top for the dawn are not drawn at all.
  • Why sheets and not a depth-aware screen pass: the board renders straight to its canvas, on demand; a screen pass needs a second render target and a depth texture, which is a change to the board, not to a kit, and a larger cost on a phone than four layers. What a screen pass would give that this does not is a perfectly soft meeting with every surface; here that is done by three things together: each sheet is a small share of the depth, the sheets roll (so no cut is a straight line), and no sheet lies within its roll of the flat top of the first step or of level-0 ground. The first pass's tint by height stays, at under half its strength, as the haze under the sheets.
  • The drawn layer is above the mist by draw order. The sheets are the first things in the see-through pass; every drawn piece (bases, marks, grid, labels, objective washes and flags, the veil, effects) is in that pass after them. Lowpoly's unlit materials are no longer changed in place: the kit dresses each lowpoly piece with its own copies (no tone mapping, see-through pass), so the first pass's fourth deviation is gone. The veil is drawn over the mist, hard-edged and hatched; mist never lies on an unseen hex.
  • Bases: the disc is drawn at 50% of its colour at dawn 0, 80% at 0.5 and full at 1 (the hue and saturation kept; a test holds that), the paper mark at about 78% at dawn 0; ink rim, selection ring and rust burst are not dimmed. The gunner (and any other lowpoly unit) now stands on the diorama's base: the kit takes lowpoly's lit base off the unit it is handed and puts its own on, the very same materials as the rifleman's.

Judged in the new stills (same headless Chromium, software WebGL, device pixel ratio 1): at dawn 0 a bank lies over D3–E5 and the water, with gaps over A3–C5; the hill stands clear; the man at E5 and the pinned man at E4 lose their legs to it while their bases, marks and the burst stay sharp; the blue and plum discs are no longer the brightest things, the amber ring and the rust bursts are. At 844 × 390 the bar and the chevron still read. Four sheets were tried by setting the desktop count to 4: no banding seen at the whole-map view or at three times closer.

Still short, after the second pass.

  • Half-swallowed is legs, not waist. The camera looks down at 55 degrees, so a standing man's chest is in front of less mist than his boots: he fades from the feet up and his outline stays fairly firm above the knee. Thicker reads as a white board, which was tried and taken back.
  • Round the board the mist looks like cloud seen from above: streaky and busy at the picture's edges, more than a table's surroundings should be.
  • Drawn on mist. Labels and the grid over a thick bank read as paint on the mist rather than on the ground under it. That is the rule asked for, and it looks a little odd in the thickest banks. Tufts under an objective wash show through as pale scratches.
  • A man lying down in a bank nearly disappears; his base and mark do not.

Not judged, second pass: a real phone and frame rate (the sheets are the new cost: up to nine, or four, see-through layers over most of the screen at dawn 0, three at dawn 1); whether a phone is really given four (the kit asks matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)'), which headless Chromium does not emulate); the mist while the camera is dragged or zoomed right in; a capture's new flag being dressed again after its effect (written, not watched).

Deviations, second pass. The fallback gunner's base is replaced, not re-lit: the kit removes the first child of the lowpoly unit (its base) and adds its own. One rifleman was added to the scene (E5), so the first-pass stills show one man fewer.

First pass.

The choices.

  • Tone mapping: ACES filmic, exposure 0.92 to 0.95 by dawn. AgX was tried on the same stills: it kept more hue in the sunlit turf but lifted the shadows and flattened the picture; ACES gives the deep blue-black of round 1 and holds the low sun at full day. The drawn layer (bases, marks, grid, labels, veil, effects) is unlit and skips tone mapping, so the palette's colours are the same in every light.
  • Mist (first pass; see above for what it is now): a few lines at the end of each world material's shader, not sheets: depth falls off with height above level-0 ground, broken up by two scales of a noise texture in world space. It is right as a still (nothing moves), costs no overdraw, has no line where a sheet would cut a hillside, and by construction never touches the drawn layer. The board stands on one big plane in the same mist, so its edge dissolves. The air has three keys (0, 0.5, 1) and is interpolated between them: sky and bounce light, a moon that becomes the sun (colour, strength, direction, shadow depth), exposure, mist colour, depth and height.
  • Grass: one physically based material for every open hex; turf, dry and damp patches (damp is darker and smoother), worn earth and a blade-length grain are made in the shader from world position, so the ground runs on across hex edges; the sides are earth with strata, grit and a ragged lip of turf. On top, per hex and seeded by its key: some forty to ninety three-bladed tufts in clumps and up to three stones, one draw call, casting no shadow.
  • The rifleman: seven rigid pieces on joints (hips, trunk with arms and rifle, head, thighs, shins), each one merged geometry of lathes, tapered limbs and boxes, painted in vertex colours with a zenithal highlight as a miniature is (lighter where a surface faces up), wool rough and steel a little metallic. Three poses, blended by the same rig lowpoly's effects drive. The base is drawn: lowpoly's colours and mark outlines, unlit; only his shadow falls on it.
  • The veil is flat near-black at 80% with fine slate hatching in world space and the hex's hard edge: unknown, not weather.

What was judged, in the stills above and others like them (headless Chromium, software WebGL, device pixel ratio 1):

  • Dawn 0 is dark, blue and foggy: the low ground lies under broken mist, the hill and the men on it stand out of it. The base colours and marks, the rings and strokes, the grid and the labels read at 1280 × 720 and at 844 × 390. At phone size the labels are small, the same size as on lowpoly, and still legible in the still.
  • Dawn 0.5 is grey with the mist off the high ground; dawn 1 has a low warm sun, long cold shadows and thin mist left in the lowest ground.
  • Unseen hexes (top right) do not read as mist: darker than anything else, hatched, hard-edged.
  • Playback: a frame caught mid-round showed the walker arrived at D4 and the shooter's flash (amber, not tone-mapped); the still after the round shows him kneeling on the burst and the man on the hill in his scrape. The real manual was opened with /?manual=dig-in&kit=diorama and drew with the kit; dev.html?kit=diorama was checked to give every frame &kit=diorama.

What was not judged.

  • No real phone, and no frame rate. Everything was software-rendered; nothing here says how it runs on a phone's GPU. The costs are behind three constants: SHADOW_MAP (2048, as lowpoly) and SKY_REFLECTION (one small sky rendered once at start, for reflections) in diorama/index.ts, and TUFTS (64 a hex, about 400 triangles and one draw call a hex) in diorama/tiles.ts. The ground shader reads the one 128 px noise texture nine times a pixel, the mist twice more. A standing rifleman is 12 draw calls (seven for the man, five for the base) before his shadow pass.
  • The motion. Headless stills show a moment, not the movement: the kneel, the fall, the lean of a dash and the sandbags rising were not watched. A unit fading in or out of sight (spotted, lost) uses lowpoly's ghost copies of the materials, which lose the mist and the paint's roughness for that quarter second: not looked at.
  • A real match with a server (the play screen's panels over this board, a 17 × 13 map, a whole squad) was not run. Device pixel ratio 2 and 3 were not tried.
  • The dawn slider's one-second ease between lights, and the two boards side by side in a real browser window.

Where it falls short of the direction.

  • The figure is a good toy, not yet a well-painted miniature. From the board's camera he is 50 to 60 px tall and reads well (helmet, webbing, rifle, the pose), but close in he is plainly capsules and boxes: no face, no hands, no folds in the cloth; his arms are fixed to the rifle, so the man lying down still holds it at the port. The kneel is a kneel, where lowpoly's pinned man lies flat: pinned and ready are told apart mainly by the rust burst.
  • The mist is paint on surfaces, not air. It has no body between the eye and the ground: a man's legs fade into it, but nothing drifts in front of him, and the sea round the board is a flat tone with blotches. At dawn 0 it is closer to blue haze than to milk lying in hollows.
  • The ground is flat-topped hexes with a good texture. The steps are still prisms; the tufts are sparse and vanish at the opening zoom; there is no relief, no mud, no puddle that really reflects. Tufts stand up through the rust burst of a pinned man.
  • Two kits in one picture (as it was in task 26; task 27 gave the kit its own woods and houses). Lowpoly's woods, house, crops, water and gunner sit in the new light and sink in the mist, but they are flat-shaded and ink-outlined beside physically based grass; the gunner's base is lit where the rifleman's is not. That is the fallback working, and it shows what is left to do.
  • The drawn layer may be too loud at dawn 0. Unlit bases in full palette colour against a moonlit board pop, as asked, but the blue and plum discs are the brightest things in the picture; they could follow the dawn a little without losing their colour.

Deviations from the task doc. (1) The grid, contour rings and labels on diorama ground are paper, not ink: ink on dark turf cannot be read at dawn 0. Same lines, same face, same sizes and places. (2) lowpoly/units.ts gained two exports and no other change: burst (it was private) and setRig, so that the new figure can use lowpoly's burst and be driven by lowpoly's effects without copying either. (3) The dev server ran on port 5199: another project's server was on 5173. (The first pass also set toneMapped on lowpoly's shared unlit materials; the second pass copies them instead.)

Next, if the direction is approved: a real phone first (frame rate decides everything else); then the figure (a sculpted mesh, arms that move, a prone pose); woods and buildings in the new materials; mist with some body (a few soft cards in the hollows at dawn 0 only); the bases' brightness tied to the dawn.
